The engagement

A safety program's question, answered with NLP

The program wanted to know whether the causes behind runway incursions could be found at scale, and whether they had shifted since a 2017 benchmark study coded by hand. We delivered two things: an NLP model that recovers the hand-coded themes and tracks them over time, and an interactive dashboard over the raw reports.

What I did

  • Built the LDA and BERTopic pipelines end to end, from raw ASRS exports to topic visualizations.
  • Wrote the narrative normalization: three rounds of aviation-abbreviation expansion so that CTAF, NOTAM and airport codes became words the models could group.
  • Tuned UMAP and HDBSCAN until BERTopic's clusters separated cleanly instead of fragmenting into near-duplicates.
  • Ran the longitudinal comparison: a 2012–2017 baseline against 2018–2025 reports, by topic and by human-factor subtype.
  • Helped translate the results into interventions and presented them to 50+ program representatives.

Why it matters

Hand-coding incursion reports is slow, limited to small samples, and easy to get inconsistent. If a model can reproduce the categories analysts already trust, the same analysis can be rerun on every new year of reports, and shifts show up as they happen instead of in the next decade's study.

Runway incursion: any unauthorized, uncoordinated, or improper entry to an active runway by an aircraft, vehicle, or person. The hold-short line in the schematic above is where most of them start.

Pipeline

From free-text narratives to comparable themes

ASRS narratives are written by pilots and controllers in their own shorthand. Most of the work was making that text consistent enough for a topic model to find structure in it.

01 · CLEAN

Merge and tokenize

Combine the 2001–2017 and 2018–2025 exports, drop irrelevant fields, lowercase, strip punctuation, tokenize and lemmatize.

02 · NORMALIZE

Expand the shorthand

Three passes: NASA's ASRS encode/decode table, then FAA and NBAA abbreviations, then ICAO and IATA airport codes. Multi-word terms become single tokens.

air_traffic controlhold short_line
03 · MODEL

LDA, then BERTopic

LDA (5 topics, 10 passes, learned α) finds co-occurring terms. BERTopic embeds each narrative with all-mpnet-base-v2, reduces with UMAP and clusters with HDBSCAN.

gensimBERTopicsentence-transformers
04 · COMPARE

Freeze, then infer

Train on the 2012–2017 baseline, then score 2018–2025 reports with the same frozen model, so a change in topic share reflects the reports and not a refit.

BERTopic configuration

Bigram-to-trigram vectorizer so topics are described by phrases rather than single words; tighter UMAP neighborhoods and a minimum cluster size of 10 so small but real themes (snow removal, non-movement areas) survive.

CountVectorizer(ngram_range=(2, 3), max_df=0.9) UMAP(n_neighbors=10, n_components=10, min_dist=0.05, metric="cosine") HDBSCAN(min_cluster_size=10, min_samples=3)

How we judged the topics

The goal was to reproduce the clusters from prior hand-coded benchmark studies, so topics were checked for coherence, top-term overlap with the benchmark categories, diversity from each other, separation on the intertopic distance map, and stability across time windows. An earlier 21-topic BERTopic run was kept for comparison; redundancy against granularity was the main trade-off.

    Limitations

    What these results don't say

    • Reports, not rates. ASRS is voluntary and confidential. A rise in a category is a rise in what was reported, which can reflect reporting culture as well as risk.
    • Narrative quality varies. Reports range from a sentence to a page, so some contribute far more text to the models than others.
    • Imbalanced categories. Human factors outnumber every other leading factor, which is why we leaned on unsupervised methods instead of a supervised classifier.
    • Ambiguous abbreviations. Expansion used a broad dictionary, but some abbreviations carry more than one meaning and were not disambiguated by context.
    • One topic per report. Each narrative is assigned its dominant topic, although incursions usually have several causes. Multi-label assignment is the obvious next step.
    • Benchmark alignment was qualitative. Topics were matched to the hand-coded categories by reading them. Jaccard or cosine overlap would make that comparison reproducible, and UMAP was tuned by hand rather than searched.
